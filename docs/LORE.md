# Endeavor: the canon

This is the story of Endeavor as it stands in the game. It is the reference every new addition is checked against.
Started 2026-11-12 at Andy's request: "every new addition consults the canon and anything outside is rejected or
changed to fit".

How to read it:
- **Canon** is true and is already in the game (a place, an object, a line of machine text, a fragment).
- **Hinted** is in the game only as a hint. Keep it a hint. Never state it in text.
- **Truth** is decided by Andy but not yet shown in the game. It is what new work builds toward. Like everything
  else, it reaches the player only through places, objects and hints, never stated in text.
- **Open** has no answer yet. Andy decides. Until then, new work must not answer it, and must not contradict the
  limits written under it.
- The source of each fact is in brackets: who decided it and where it shows in the game.

## The rules for new work (read before adding anything)
1. Read this file before you add a place, a character, an item, a line of text or a system with a story reason.
2. Check the addition against every **Canon**, **Truth** and **Hinted** line. If it contradicts one, change the addition to fit,
   or drop it. Do not quietly change the canon to fit the addition.
3. If an addition needs a new fact, write the fact here in the same commit, marked **Canon** (if the game now shows
   it) or **Hinted**, with its source. If the fact answers an **Open** question, stop and ask Andy first.
4. Show, don't tell (Andy, 2026-10-31). The game never explains its lore in a banner or a tutorial. The story is told
   through places, objects, terse machine text ("> STACK RESIDENT . . . REBOOTING"), contact lines and fragments.
   Contacts and fragments hint; they never state.
5. Everything is original to Endeavor. Draw on genres, never on a named game's characters, art, places or story.
6. Names and words in the game are fixed once used. Use the glossary below; do not invent synonyms.

## Glossary (the game's own words)
- **Conduit**: a player in Endeavor. A person who lives as an uploaded consciousness in a body. [Andy 2026-10-31]
- **Stack**: a conduit's uploaded consciousness. It can be "resident" (uploaded) or "none". [Andy 2026-10-31]
- **Reboot**: what death is for a conduit whose stack is uploaded. [Andy 2026-10-31; death screen]
- **Recruit**: a clone soldier on the Front that a conduit's stack links into. [Front]
- **Link / unlink**: a conduit's stack joining a recruit / leaving it. [Front machine text]
- **Neural Net**: the terminals in the city that link a conduit to the Front ("tap in"). [NeuralNet terminals]
- **The hour / the cycle**: the hourly reset of the world. Never called "reset" in the game's text. [Cycle]
- **Line 9**: the train line that brings stack hardware up into the city. [Claude 2026-11-10; the arrival]
- **Stack hardware**: what Line 9 carries: new bodies, and the tanks recruits grow in. [Claude 2026-11-10; crates]
- **Bay**: a hideout site, seen as a Line 9 delivery point ("LINE 9 . BAY 07"). [Claude 2026-11-10]
- **Fragment**: a recovered piece of the truth, read into the stack's ARCHIVE. [Clues]

## The world

**Canon**
- The planet is almost all industry and pollution. Green survives only in a few small pockets. [Andy 2026-11-10;
  the megacity, the valley]
- The megacity is a ruined cyberpunk city: a core of four districts (Transit Hub, Corp Plaza, The Sprawl, Blackout
  Zone) around the Crossroads, ringed by industrial districts (Scrap Sea, The Foundry, The Sumps, Slag Flats, The Hab
  Blocks, The Stacks, Pylon Fields). Toxic sludge burns. [megacity]
- The city's green pockets are The Last Garden, The Rootwork and Fernwell. They heal the body. [megacity]
- North of the city wall is a dead valley, reached by the Scarp's switchbacks, with Verdant Reach hidden in it: the
  one living settlement, with a market. [frontier]
- The world is wrecked but inhabited: raiders, Brutes, Shockers, Wardens, rival conduit crews. [NPCs]
- Most people with money uploaded their stacks and left. The conduits are the ones still here. [Andy 2026-10-31]

**Truth**
- Conduits try to leave, and fail. The only routes out they have ever found drop them into the Front. Every
  expedition beyond the city ends in a reset: they can never get far enough before they need to extract.
  [Andy 2026-11-12] (Read with the game as it stands: the valley north of the wall is as far as anyone gets; the
  border peaks are where expeditions turn back or end. Nothing in the game may show a conduit getting further.)

**Hinted**
- The rich who "left" did not go anywhere physical. Fragment "The Departed": a receipt for one stack, one upload,
  one exit, signed by a name on a city tower that nobody living has.

## The stack and death

**Canon**
- A conduit uploads its stack once, through the hideout computer. That first log-in is initialization
  ("> INIT 1/1 . . . STACK READ . . . RESIDENT"). From then on the computer runs on the conduit. [Andy 2026-10-31]
- With an uploaded stack, death is a reboot: what the stack knows survives (contacts, contracts, the ARCHIVE), and the
  conduit wakes at its hideout computer ("> STACK RESIDENT . . . REBOOTED"). [Andy 2026-10-31; Claude 2026-11-10]
- Without an upload, death is the end of that conduit: "> NO STACK . . . NO RECOVERY". Everything the body carried
  and knew is lost. A new body arrives up Line 9. [Andy 2026-10-31; Claude 2026-11-10]
- The body is always lost on death. Gear drops where it falls, for anyone to take. Bodies stay. [permadeath]
- The hideout and the route north belong to the crew and outlive any one conduit. [Andy 2026-10-31]
- Contacts, the computer's apps and fragments are learned by meeting people in the world. [Andy 2026-10-31]

## Line 9 and the arrival (Claude's links, 2026-11-10; Andy may revise)

**Canon**
- A new conduit arrives riding a Line 9 train, cloaked and armed, into a dead station at SUBLEVEL 3. The station is
  held by sentries. At its bottom the CONTROL room holds the line's manifest. [the arrival]
- The manifest: "INBOUND: 1 . OUTBOUND: 0 / CARGO: STACK HARDWARE, 40 CRATES / DELIVERY: FREIGHT LIFTS, CITY BAYS
  01-12 / CONSIGNEE: WHOEVER SIGNS / NOTE: NOTHING ON THIS LINE COMES BACK DOWN." [Config.Tutorial.Manifest]
- A service lift takes the conduit up into the city, into a freight lift at one of the twelve bays. Each bay is a
  hideout site. Unclaimed bays hold crates stencilled STACK HARDWARE. [freight lifts]
- Signing the manifest at a bay claims it; its crates become the conduit's computer. [freight lifts]
- Line 9 runs one way: up. Nothing goes back down. [manifest]
- Guns take real calibres, and a cartridge fits every gun chambered for it. The conduit arrives with a suppressed
  .22 pistol (the Whisper): a small, very quiet round that only kills through flesh. The station's sentries wear
  steel helmets and plate, and carry 9mm or 5.56, the common pistol and rifle calibres; none of them carries .22.
  [Andy 2026-11-12]

**Hinted**
- New conduits are themselves the stack hardware Line 9 delivers. (The manifest counts one inbound passenger among
  the cargo; the Broker: "Forty crates. Never forty-one.")

## The corporations and the underground (Andy, 2026-11-12)

**Truth**
- Corporations own Line 9, as they own everything. [Andy 2026-11-12]
- The corporations fund the Front's endless war, on both sides. [Andy 2026-11-12]
- Most corporations operate out of bomb shelters deep underground. [Andy 2026-11-12]
- The cloning facilities are in those shelters: the recruits' tanks, and the stack hardware that Line 9 carries up,
  come from there. [Andy 2026-11-12; fits Line 9 and the tank plates]
- The shelters also hold the barrens: halls where the corporations keep cybernetic brain slaves, wired to screens,
  making power with their own hands. [Andy 2026-11-12]
- The world the conduits experience seems to run on the power the brain slaves make. Conduits and slaves live in
  symbiosis: once a conduit tried to free them, and lost the connection to his stack. [Andy 2026-11-12]
- Players can go down to the shelters and the barrens. Something in the game leads them there, either an incentive
  or the story. [Andy 2026-11-12] Limit from the canon: the way down is not Line 9 (it runs up only, and nothing
  goes back down on it).

**Canon (Endeavor v94, Claude's build of the way down; Andy chose "story and incentive")**
- A service shaft on the Sumps plaza ("SERVICE . S4 . NO ENTRY", a winch, a red lamp, a hum through the grate)
  drops to Barrens Hall 2 in Shelter 4: a pipe gallery, the hall, and the overseers' office. [Barrens]
- In the hall the brain slaves stand at stations in rows: pale figures working cranks, each wired from the head into
  a trunk cable, each with a screen reading its OUTPUT. Overseers patrol; the office console counts HANDS and
  OUTPUT. The office stores hold implants and cells, more than anything in the city. [Barrens]
- Unhooking a slave is never a clean win: the figure slumps, the lights of its row go out, the overseers hear it,
  and the conduit's own link kicks back. It loses health, and an uploaded stack loses its upload ("> STACK . . . NO
  CARRIER") until the conduit logs in at its computer again. [Barrens; fits the symbiosis above]
- The hour reaches the barrens like everywhere outside the green pockets: every hour the slave is back at its crank.
  [Barrens]
- Signs down there name places (S4, Barrens, Hall 2), never a corporation (open question 2).

**Hinted**
- Fragment "Shift Card" (by the shaft head): a punch card for S4 . BARRENS . HALL 2, clocked in every hour, never
  out. Fragment "Hands" (in the hall, after the card): "he came down to cut them loose. he got one. the lights went
  out in both of them. he never found his way back in." (Andy's conduit who lost his stack.)
- The Broker feels something under the Sumps plaza; the Fixer says the Sumps drain people who are curious; the
  Dealer's best lenses "came up a shaft in the Sumps. I didn't ask whose they were."

**Canon (already in the game, now explained by the Truth above)**
- Line 9 comes up from below the city (the station is at SUBLEVEL 3), and nothing goes back down. [the arrival]
- The manifest's consignee is withheld, and the Front Dispatch is stamped by both factions at once. [Config, Clues]

**Hinted (how this may reach the player)**
- Corporate marks on Line 9 hardware, the tanks and the Front's supply crates, never with a company explained.
- The barrens only through traces: power lines running down, a fragment, a contact who will not talk about it.

## The hour (the cycle)

**Canon**
- Every hour the world dematerializes and comes back exactly as it was: everything broken is whole again, loose
  remains are gone, contacts stand in new places and say new lines. People stay where they stand with what they
  carry. Hideouts are untouched. [Cycle]
- It shows itself: debris flickers, colour drains, a second of white. No countdown, no explanation. [Andy 2026-10-31]
- The green pockets stand outside the hour: colour does not drain there, and what lies there is not cleared.
  [Claude 2026-11-10]

**Truth**
- In the green pockets grass grows, trees and flowers bloom, and the hour's effects are nullified, for reasons
  nobody knows. [Andy 2026-11-12]

**Truth**
- There is some kind of glitch or disconnection between reality and what the conduits experience. Little more is
  known, and more is to be uncovered as the game goes on. [Andy 2026-11-12]
- The green pockets are where the hour does not reach (see above). Andy's guess, not settled: the connection to the
  old earth may be strongest there. [Andy 2026-11-12]

**Hinted**
- The world is not what it looks like. It may be a held, repeating record. Fragments: "Hour Zero" ("RESET HOLDS.
  SUBJECTS UNAWARE. BEGIN AGAIN AT HOUR ZERO."), "Lamp Count", "The Gatekeeper" (a guard who always stands back up
  facing the city), "Seed Packet" (a strain named for tomorrow's date), "Pressed", "The Door" ("outside the hour").
- The Neural Net does not really run to the Front; it runs "up" to something labelled ARCHIVE (fragment "Uplink").
- Hideouts keep through the hour, and the Fixer wonders why.

## The Front

**Canon**
- The Front is a separate war world, Cold War in look and weapons: Union (blue) against Pact (red), with no rounds and
  no end. A moving hill and the ever-tipping ticket scales decide who is winning. [Andy; Front]
- A conduit taps in at a Neural Net terminal and its stack links into a recruit: a clone grown in a stasis tank. The
  tanks are Line 9 stack hardware ("LINE 9 . STACK HARDWARE . nn/40"). [Front; Claude 2026-11-10]
- The first link: the recruit wakes panicking in the tank, remembers the battlefield in flashes ("> STACK SYNC"),
  drops down a chute into a barracks where the conduit's kit waits, and trains in a wireframe simulation of a city
  before it is sent to the field. [Andy 2026-11-10; Front intro]
- Recruits die for good. Their gear drops damaged. Their bodies calcify where they fall, and the dead build the hills
  the war is fought over. [Andy 2026-11-10]
- The conduit is not hurt by a recruit's death; it costs gear. The conduit can unlink at its own base or between
  recruits and goes back to the city, to the terminal it left from. [Andy 2026-10-31; Claude 2026-11-10]
- What the Front gives back is credits, spent in the city (the Reach's trader, the hideout computer's HOUSE and
  MARKET). [Recruit, Terminal]
- One glade on the Front is the last green there. [Andy 2026-11-10; Front world]

**Truth**
- The Front has been forgotten by progress. It has looked the way it does now since the 1970s. Nothing is made
  there: everything on the Front (weapons, kit, tanks, recruits) arrived by shipment. [Andy 2026-11-12]
- The Front's guns are real Cold War patterns (AK, AR, Garand and M14, 1911, service revolver), and they come as
  parts: a locker order can fit a different barrel, muzzle device or sight to the same receiver. A few later arms
  (the Glock 17) are there too, and the Garands there are chambered in 7.62x51; like everything else they arrived by shipment, and neither side makes them.
  [Andy 2026-11-12: "All the cold war era weapons ... should be remade in game, like the glock, ak47, m1 garand"]
- The conduits supply the Front. Each conduit can link into, and supply, as many recruits as it can afford.
  [Andy 2026-11-12; fits the recruit locker, where a conduit orders recruits and kit with its own credits]

**Hinted**
- The war is managed, not fought to be won. Fragment "Front Dispatch", stamped by both factions: "Ticket scales reset
  to balance. Recruit losses: acceptable. Conduit retention: nominal." The Dealer: "Recruits come back as hills. You
  come back as you. Somebody's paying for the difference."

## The people

**Canon**
- Three contacts, met in the world, each brings an app online on the hideout computer:
  - The Broker (at the Verdant Market): RAIDS, contracts on sites, bounties and the Blackout raid.
  - The Fixer (under the Crossroads span): HOUSE, materials for the hideout.
  - The Dealer (on the Scarp Overlook): MARKET, cybernetics "pulled from people who no longer need them".
- Contacts change place and line every hour. A few of their lines slip and show they half-notice the hour.
  [Contacts, Clues]
- Rival conduit crews work the same contracts out in the valley. [Frontier]
- The Gatekeeper guards the Scarp Overlook and has died there countless times. [Frontier, fragment]

## Power in the body

**Canon**
- Conduits fight with flickering cursed energy: timed strikes chain into a Black Flash and the Zone. [Andy]
- Cybernetics replace eyes, ears, nose, core, arms and legs. They are ripped in and out on an Autonet table at a
  hideout, at a cost in blood. [cybernetics]
- Conduits can devour the remains of the dead for strength. [Andy]

**Open**
- What cursed energy is in this world, and why conduits have it. Limit: it belongs to conduits' bodies; never give it
  to recruits on the Front.

## Open questions (Andy decides; new work must not answer these)
1. What the glitch behind the hour is, beyond "a disconnection between reality and what conduits experience"
   (Andy, 2026-11-12: "more can be uncovered as we go"). Limit: never confirm it in text. Fragments may only add
   evidence, one piece at a time.
2. Which corporations, by name, and who signs as the withheld consignee. (That corporations run Line 9 and fund the
   Front is now Truth.) Limit: nobody in the city can name them yet.
3. Where "up" goes, and what the departed rich became. Limit: no one who left is ever met.
4. How shipments reach the Front, and why it was left in the 1970s. (That it is forgotten by progress, supplied
   only by shipment, and that the conduits supply it, is now Truth.) Limit: the two worlds must stay visibly different eras; nothing modern reaches
   the Front.
5. What the green pockets really are, and whether Andy's guess (the connection to the old earth is strongest there)
   is right. Limit: they stay rare, small and quiet; no text confirms the guess.
6. What stops conduits from getting far enough, and what lies beyond. (That they try and always fail is now Truth.)
   Limit: no exit exists in the game; a conduit never gets further than the valley.
7. Whether the world truly runs on the slaves' power ("seems to"), and what freeing them would do beyond cutting a
   stack's connection. (The symbiosis is now Truth.) Limit: freeing them is never a clean win for a conduit.
8. Whether there are other ways down, and what else the shelters hold (the corporations' own floors, the cloning
   facilities). (One way down, the Sumps shaft to Barrens Hall 2, is now Canon.) Limit: not by Line 9.
